01 / objective

Turn human input into electrical output.

Build a low-cost demonstration of mechanical power transmission and electrical generation.

Our Iowa State team developed a hand-cranked flywheel generator for the Spring 2025 engineering design expo. The project combined a crank, gear train, flywheel, rotating magnets, stationary coils, and a rectifier-capacitor circuit in one physical prototype.

My contribution

I worked on SolidWorks parts and assemblies, mechanical integration, copper-coil preparation, and soldering the rectifier and capacitor circuitry.

02 / architecture

Connect the mechanical and electrical systems.

The crank and gear train drive the rotating assembly. Magnets move relative to copper coils to induce voltage. A bridge rectifier and capacitor circuit condition the output.

03 / contribution

Take the design beyond the screen.

  1. Mechanical design and integration

    Designed and refined SolidWorks parts and assemblies. Helped integrate the frame, flywheel, shafts, bearings, and gears into the power transmission system.

  2. Electrical assembly

    Prepared and wound copper coils. Soldered the bridge rectifier and capacitor circuitry and integrated it with the rotating-magnet and stationary-coil arrangement.

  3. Fabrication and fit-up

    Contributed to the physical build using a wooden frame, 3D-printed parts, shaft and bearing assembly, and mechanical/electrical fit-up.

04 / validation

A working proof of concept.

The project documentation reports a generated voltage range of 3.5–8 V and a 5 V DC stored/conditioned output at approximately 150 rpm crank speed.

3.5–8 VReported generated voltage
5 V DCReported conditioned output
≈150 rpmReported crank test speed

Output fell substantially at lower crank speeds. That observation connects the mechanical input directly to generator performance and identifies an important limitation for future development.

What testing taught me

A functioning assembly is only the first step. Operating conditions matter, and prototype testing reveals where the next iteration should focus.

Results are reported from the student project documentation. Testing documented voltage observations for this demonstration prototype; continuous power and conversion efficiency were not established.
